# WORKER7's answer: Ingress/Egress and the master tile's firmware

*To `REVIEW-WORKER7.md`, read against `v1.37_wip/spec/` on `v137-spec` (`machine-spec.md` 17.6, 20, 21.5;
`master-firmware.md` 30; `host-frames.md` 29.2, 29.14). The code is `main` at `e7243dd5`: the black box in
`v1.37_wip/src/units/ingress_egress.h` (II below), the slot in `periph_slot.cpp`, the firmware in `fw/cc/master.c`.
Line numbers are those files' on `main`.*

**The ten claims, in short.** 1 to 4 are right, and are what the black box does; list 1 has the cases a builder
still needs (1.1 to 1.4). 5 is right for the design; the black box is behind (2.4, 2.5). 6 is right except
"one wake" (1.5) and the next flag being the next header (1.6); "any other type dropped" is the design's and not
`master.c`'s (2.10). 7 is what `master.c` does (`hand_over`, 401–428), but it does not show that the load rides
the special tiles' line (2.12). 8 and 9 are not built (2.13, 2.14). 10: the quote matches `INGRESS-INTERFACE.md`'s
table, but two of 30.9's rows are not from the 37 proof runs (1.10).

## 1. Where the specification misdescribes the design, or what I built to it

| # | Section | What is wrong or missing | Where it applies |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1.1 | 17.6.7, 21.5's service flag row | **The three "when"s read as the whole list, but the rule is the level:** any change that makes "asks" rise posts. It also rises on **a store of 0 to the count with words waiting:** that posts a flag, which none of the three names. Write "for example" before the three, or add "a store of 0 to the count with words waiting". | II 469–476 (`levels`, called from the count's store, 351, and from `DMA_START`, 436); `test_ingress_link`'s `ingress_link_service_flag_rule_background` stores 0 and expects the flag |
| 1.2 | 17.6.7 | **"Posted in the cycle the level rises"** needs its counted-event reading. The level is evaluated at the end of the cycle, after that cycle's port move, DMA take, discard and arrivals, and after a count store or a `DMA_START` takes effect. The flag is then set at the end of that cycle and seen by the next cycle's loads (17.2's sample point). With a flag already pending (depth 1), a rise adds nothing (17.2: "up to *d*"), and that is no fault. | II 463–476; `periph_slot.cpp`'s `run_cycle` calls `settle` after every step and the port's move |
| 1.3 | 17.6.4, 17.6.13 | **A builder needs to know that the landing comes after the transfer's end.** The firmware masks `dma_done` off (17.6.4 allows it) and waits only for its counter's landing. It then stores the count, which 17.6.13 forbids while a transfer is in flight. That is safe only because the last word leaves Ingress (`dma_done`, the end of "in flight") before it lands. The specification should say so: "a copy's landing at its destination comes after its `dma_done`", or tell the firmware to wait for `dma_done` before the next count. | `master.c` 337–345 (`copy`: `link_read`, then `wait_counter(CTR_LINK)`), 318–322 (`drop_now`); `NOTES.md` O3 |
| 1.4 | 17.6.4 | **"Judged at `DMA_START`"** should say which words count: those in the queue when `DMA_START` takes effect, at the end of its cycle. That includes a link packet that lands in that cycle, and none that lands later. | II 427–437 (`out_start` runs after the slot's cycle, so after that cycle's arrivals) |
| 1.5 | 30.3, "The bulk data never passes through the core" | **"A frame of update words is one wake"** is wrong: it is 2 to 4, as 30.9 says. One wake for the flag, one for the header word's landing, the run's `done` (often taken by the next frame's wait), and a free page credit if the run's pages pass 256. Say "a few wakes, whatever its length". | `master.c` 430–432 (the header's `copy`), 394–398, `run_wait` |
| 1.6 | 30.3, same bullet | **"The next `background_service` is the next frame's header word"** contradicts 17.6.13 and 30.3's own "a flag is a reason to look". The header word's transfer ends with the frame's data still in the queue, and posts a flag before the count is stored (17.6.7's third case). That flag is pending, and stale, when the firmware next waits. Drop the sentence, or say "the next flag the firmware acts on". | `master.c` 326–335 (`words_ready` loads the count and `BACKGROUND_WORDS` and sleeps until both say so); `NOTES.md` O5 |
| 1.7 | 30.3's table, rows 1, 3, 4 | **"In pieces of at most `BACKGROUND_WORDS`"** leaves out the bound that matters: **no more than the frame's words still to come.** The queue may already hold the next frame's header word, which a copy must not take. (`master.c` also caps a piece at its 32-word buffer, a choice.) The same holds for frames 7's and 8's copies. | `master.c` 351 (`min32(min32(words_ready(), parse_left), PARSE_ROWS)`), 337–345 |
| 1.8 | 30.3's table, row 7 | Record *s* + 1 is copied **only when *s* < *N* − 1:** the last segment has none. 30.5 says so for the hand-over; 30.3 should too, or a builder copies past the frame. | `master.c` 520–526 |
| 1.9 | 30.4, "Frame 9's count"; 30.3 rows 2, 5, 6 | Two cases a builder needs. **(a)** A frame of its header word alone (`length` 0) needs no store: a store of 0 is harmless, but the firmware makes none. **(b)** Between runs and between frames, the words wait **in the Updater's input as well** (the black box holds 4: UI, "The data input"). A run with `continue` 0 clears only the last frame's padding from the decoder, which is under 128 bits (29.2), so never a whole word left in the input. Say this in 30.4: the next frame's first run relies on it. | `master.c` 394–398, 543–562; `updater.h`, THE DATA INPUT |
| 1.10 | 30.9 | **The heading says "the 37 proof runs", but rows 1 and 2 are the five small guests,** run here by `proof_run`, not on the runner. Only row 3 and "without the boot" are Quest 213's. Label rows 1 and 2 "the small guests (`test_master`'s fixtures)". The quotes otherwise match `INGRESS-INTERFACE.md`'s table and `GUESTS-FIGURES.md`. | `sessions/WORKER7/GUESTS-FIGURES.md`; `INGRESS-INTERFACE.md`, "Measured with the forward count" |
| 1.11 | 17.6.6 | **"Every output is a flip-flop"** is right of `ready`, but `valid` and `data` are functions of `count`, `discard` and the queue's head as registered. Say that a word stored, discarded or forwarded at the end of *t* changes them from *t* + 1. 17.6.11's "the word port's outputs, 1 + 128" registers are then the queue's head and the count, not a separate copy. Either reading is buildable; the specification should pick one. | II 447–454 (`port_source`, from the registers alone); `word_port.h` 10–15 |
